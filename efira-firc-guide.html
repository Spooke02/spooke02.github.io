e-FIRA and FIRC for Indian freelancers: a plain guide

If clients abroad pay you in dollars, euros or pounds, you will keep hearing about an e-FIRA or FIRC. Here is what that is and how to keep your paperwork in order.

What it is

A FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate) or an e-FIRA (electronic Foreign Inward Remittance Advice) is a document that records a foreign payment arriving in your Indian bank account: who sent it, how much, in what currency, the rupee amount credited, and the date. Banks issue the paper FIRC; some regulated payment platforms issue the electronic e-FIRA.

Why freelancers keep one for every payment

  • It is proof that the money came from abroad, which supports treating your work as an export of services.
  • It shows the exact rupee value on the day, useful when you file returns or if you register for GST.
  • Banks, CAs and sometimes loan or visa officers ask for it.

How to get one

  1. Ask your bank. Many banks issue a FIRC on request for a foreign inward remittance. Fees and time vary, and some sources mention a few hundred rupees per remittance and several working days.
  2. Use a platform that issues an e-FIRA for each payment, if you receive money through one. Check what it provides before you rely on it.
  3. Marketplaces and wallets differ. Some withdrawals produce only a remittance advice, not a full FIRC/e-FIRA. Ask your bank which document you will actually get for the way you withdraw.

Common mistakes

  • Name mismatch. The name on the platform account should match your bank account holder, or the bank may reject the credit.
  • Not asking until later. Request the document soon after the money arrives, not months later at filing time.
  • Mixing payments. Keep one record per payment, with the date, currency, amount, fee and the rupee amount credited.
  • Guessing the rate. Use the rate shown on your document. Our converter gives an ECB reference estimate for planning only.

A simple checklist

  • Log each foreign payment the day it lands: client, platform, currency, amount, fee, INR credited.
  • Request the e-FIRA/FIRC and note the date you asked.
  • File each document with its payment record.
  • Watch your running total against the GST registration threshold.
  • Review everything with a Chartered Accountant before filing.
