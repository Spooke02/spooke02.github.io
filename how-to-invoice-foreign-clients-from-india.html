How to invoice foreign clients from India

A practical checklist for a clean, professional invoice and the records you should keep afterwards.

What to put on the invoice

  • Your name or business name, address and contact details.
  • The client's name and address (and their tax ID if they ask for it).
  • A unique invoice number and the invoice date.
  • A clear description of the work, the quantity or hours, and the rate.
  • The currency and the total, stated clearly.
  • Payment terms (for example, due in 15 days) and how to pay.

Currency and payment details

Invoice in the currency you agreed with the client. Put in only the payment details the client needs (for example, a PayPal email, or bank details if you use a transfer service). Never put passwords or card numbers on an invoice. Platform and bank fees come out of what you receive, so note the amount that actually reaches you.

GST and export of services

If you are GST-registered, an invoice for an export of services may need special wording and may be linked to a Letter of Undertaking. If you are not registered, you generally do not charge GST. Which case applies to you is something to confirm with a Chartered Accountant. See also: do freelancers need GST registration?

After you are paid

  • Record the date, the currency amount, the fee and the rupees credited. The USD to INR converter gives an ECB reference estimate for planning.
  • Ask your bank or platform for the e-FIRA or FIRC and file it with the payment; see the guide.
  • Follow up politely if payment is late; the reminder message generator can help.

Tip

Agree scope, price, payment dates and a part-payment up front in writing. Most late-payment problems start with an unclear agreement.
